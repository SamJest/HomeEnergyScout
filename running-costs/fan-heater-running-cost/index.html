Fan heater running cost

Fan heaters warm a person or small room quickly, but common 2kW models can become expensive if they run for long sessions.

Quick answer

A 2kW fan heater costs about £0.53 per hour at full power using 26.32p/kWh electricity.

Last reviewed: 2026-11-01 UK-focused estimate Uses current Ofgem benchmark rate

The practical answer

Best for quick bursts and short warm-up periods; less attractive for long steady heating in a poor-fit room.

Sources and assumptions

  • Ofgem energy price cap unit rates and standing charges: Ofgem price cap, 1 October to 31 December 2026.
  • Electricity rate used in tables and default calculators: 26.32p/kWh.
  • Standing charges are not added to appliance-only examples because you usually pay them whether or not this heater is used.
  • Figures are estimates, not supplier quotes or installer advice.
  • Checked 2026-11-01. Electricity is zero-rated for VAT; gas includes 5% VAT. GB averages do not cover Northern Ireland.
  • Standing charges: electricity 54.83p/day; gas 29.68p/day, excluded here.
  • Gas unit price is not delivered-heat cost: divide by boiler seasonal efficiency. Heat pumps use a different efficiency model. Equal expenditure does not imply equal comfort.

Formula used

(watts / 1000) x hours x electricity price per kWh

For this page's 2kW example, 3 hours at full output costs £1.58. Used the same way for 30 days, it costs £47.38; at 65% average output it is closer to £30.79.

How to use the result

  • Use your own tariff if it differs from 26.32p/kWh.
  • For thermostat-controlled heaters, compare full power with a lower average-output scenario.
  • If the cost looks high, check room size and insulation before assuming a different heater type solves it.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions this page is built to solve.

Are fan heaters cheap to run?

They can be cheap for short bursts, but a common 2kW fan heater costs about £0.53 per hour at full power.

Why does a fan heater feel expensive?

It often heats quickly but loses comfort quickly after switching off, so people may run it for longer or more often than expected.
